Task 1: Find and contact your academic advisor

Academic advisors can help with course scheduling, degree requirements, registration problems, and other academic concerns.

  1. Open the advisor directory.

    Use the College of Computing Undergraduate Academic Advising Team directory.

  2. Match your major and last-name range.

    Read the advisor descriptions carefully because assignments are organized by program and last name.

  3. Schedule through Navigate360.

    Use the appointment link in the advisor directory or open Navigate360 through BuzzPort.

  4. Prepare a specific question.

    Include your GTID when requested and bring your current course plan, Degree Works information, or the exact registration error you received.

Task 2: Check degree progress and plan future courses

Use multiple sources together. Degree Works provides a personalized audit, while College pages and the catalog provide program requirements, prerequisite maps, and course-planning materials.

Which source should you use?
Question Best starting point What to check
What requirements have I completed? Registrar degree information and Degree Works Completed, in-progress, and remaining requirements
What are the CS core and Thread requirements? College of Computing undergraduate degree information Core requirements, Thread combination sheets, prerequisite maps, and anticipated offerings
What does a course require? Georgia Tech catalog: undergraduate CS courses Course description, credit hours, and listed prerequisites
Before meeting with an advisor: Draft at least two possible course plans. Mark any prerequisite, Thread, or graduation questions that you cannot answer from the official materials.

Task 3: Prepare for registration

The Registrar assigns each student a time ticket that controls when they can add or drop courses. The College of Computing also maintains a registration page with CS-specific information and updates.

1–2 weeks before

  • Review Degree Works and prerequisites.
  • Draft a first-choice schedule.
  • Create backup course options.

A few days before

  • Check your time ticket.
  • Check for registration holds.
  • Read current CS registration updates.

When registration opens

  • Register for priority courses first.
  • Join available waitlists when needed.
  • Record any error message exactly.

After registering

  • Recheck your complete schedule.
  • Confirm credit hours and linked labs.
  • Contact advising about unresolved issues.

Registration links

  • Read current College of Computing undergraduate registration information
  • Open the Registrar’s registration resource page
  • View official registration guides and videos
  • Review time-ticket information

Quick-reference checklist

Use this list at the beginning of a semester or before registration.

  • I know who my assigned academic advisor is.
  • I know how to schedule an appointment in Navigate360.
  • I reviewed Degree Works for missing requirements.
  • I checked my Thread and core requirements.
  • I reviewed prerequisites for planned courses.
  • I created at least two backup schedules.
  • I checked my time ticket and registration holds.
  • I read current CS registration updates.
  • I know where to find tutoring and office-hour support.
  • I saved links to the resources I use most often.

Common questions

I found conflicting information. Which source should I trust?

Use the most recent official College of Computing or Registrar page, then ask your assigned advisor when the information affects your degree progress or registration.

I received a registration error. What should I send to advising?

Record the exact error message, course and section, term, and the steps you already attempted. Avoid sending only a screenshot without a text description because text is easier to search and more accessible.

Why does this guide avoid links labeled “here”?

Screen-reader users may navigate through a list of links without hearing the surrounding paragraph. Descriptive link wording explains the destination by itself.

Sources and maintenance notes

This training guide uses official Georgia Tech materials. Because registration rules, advisor assignments, and support programs may change, future editors should verify each link at least once per semester.
